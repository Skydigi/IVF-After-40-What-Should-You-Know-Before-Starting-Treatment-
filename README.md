# IVF After 40: What to Know

A short guide to fertility testing, egg quality and IVF planning for women over 40.

> ⚠️ **Medical note:** This guide is for general education only. Talk to a fertility specialist about your own situation.

---

## Is IVF After 40 Possible?

**Yes.** IVF can be considered after 40, but planning usually needs a more detailed evaluation. Fertility declines with age, so ASRM recommends that **women over 40 get evaluated and treated sooner** rather than waiting.

Age alone doesn't decide your outcome. The fertility team also looks at:

- Ovarian reserve
- Menstrual and ovulation history
- Previous pregnancies
- Previous IVF or fertility treatment
- Uterine and tubal factors
- Sperm quality
- Overall medical history

---

## Why Fertility Changes After 40

With age, both the **number and the quality of eggs** go down. This can affect:

- How many eggs (and how many mature eggs) are retrieved
- Fertilisation and embryo development
- Chromosomal status of embryos
- Miscarriage risk

According to ASRM, the chances of pregnancy and live birth fall with age, while the risks of aneuploidy and miscarriage rise.

---

## Ovarian Reserve Tests

| Test | What It Shows |
|---|---|
| **AMH** | Ovarian reserve and likely response to stimulation |
| **Antral Follicle Count (AFC)** | Ultrasound count of small follicles in the ovaries |
| **FSH & other hormones** | Ordered depending on your clinical situation |

> **AMH is not a pregnancy test.** ASRM notes that AMH and AFC predict egg yield well but are only weakly linked to egg quality, pregnancy and live birth.

---

## Tests Before IVF

Depending on your history, these may include:

- AMH, AFC and other hormone tests
- Pelvic ultrasound and uterine assessment
- Tubal evaluation (when relevant)
- Semen analysis
- Review of past pregnancies and fertility treatment
- Genetic testing or counselling (when indicated)

Testing should fit your history, not follow a one-size-fits-all package.

---

## IVF With Your Own Eggs

This **can be considered** after 40, but outcomes depend strongly on age and other individual factors.

> The CDC cautions that national or clinic success-rate averages **don't represent your personal chance of success**. Discuss your own situation with your specialist rather than relying on percentages found online.

---

## Egg Quality vs. Egg Quantity

As you get older, a larger share of your eggs may carry chromosomal abnormalities. This can lower embryo potential and raise miscarriage risk.

**Quantity ≠ quality.** You can have a reasonable ovarian reserve and still have age-related changes in egg quality, so a good evaluation looks at both.

---

## Key Takeaway

IVF after 40 should be **personalised**, based on a full evaluation rather than age alone.
